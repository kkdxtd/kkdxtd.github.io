## Overview

This is my primary M.Eng. research project at USTC, supervised by Assoc. Prof. Fenghua Qin. The goal is to understand how **tandem undulating fins** — inspired by ribbon-fin fish like *Apteronotus albifrons* — generate thrust and how fin–fin hydrodynamic interactions affect overall propulsion efficiency.

## Motivation

Undulating fins offer several advantages over traditional propellers in underwater vehicles:
- High maneuverability at low speeds
- Simultaneous forward/backward thrust without reversing rotation
- Quiet operation suitable for sensitive environments

However, the complex unsteady flow field generated by these fins, especially in multi-fin (tandem) configurations, is not yet well understood.

## Methodology & Multi-Solver Framework

- **Multi-Solver Exploration:** Evaluated and compared multiple CFD approaches, including commercial finite-volume methods (ANSYS Fluent with custom UDFs for dynamic mesh generation), an in-house AMR solver (IAMReX with Immersed Boundary Method), and Lattice Boltzmann methods.
- **Primary CFD Architecture:** Open-source Lattice Boltzmann Method (LBM) framework **Palabos**, integrating Large Eddy Simulation (LES, Smagorinsky subgrid-scale model) and iterative velocity-correction Immersed Boundary Method (IBM).
- **Simulation Models:** Both 2D cross-sectional slice models (for efficient parametric exploration) and full 3D long-fin models.
- **Key Parameters Studied:**
  - Inter-fin spacing ($\Delta x / L$)
  - Initial phase difference ($\Delta \phi$) between anterior and posterior fins
  - Kinematic parameters (wavelength, frequency, undulation amplitude)
- **Key Theoretical Contribution:** Formulated an analytical **Equivalent Phase Model** ($R^2 > 0.97$) unifying spatial vortex convection delay and temporal actuation phase shift, capturing optimal energy-harvesting configurations where posterior fin thrust exceeds that of an isolated fin.
